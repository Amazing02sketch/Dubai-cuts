Nocturne builds a small number of mechanical watches each year, regulated by hand under low light, in a workshop that keeps no clocks of its own. Étienne Roussel, Founder The Collection Three movements, one philosophy. Each piece is produced in a run of fewer than forty. None are reissued. Price on request Ref. 03 — Veilleuse Minute repeater Chimes the hour on demand, tuned by ear against a tuning fork, never a machine. Price on request The Atelier Four stages. No shortcuts between them. 01 Sketch The movement is drawn before the case — proportion is decided in graphite, not gold. 02 Cast Each case is poured individually; small variations in the gold are left visible, not polished away. 03 Assemble Around two hundred parts are set by hand under a loupe, over several unhurried days. 04 Regulate The finished movement is timed across six positions before it is permitted to leave the bench. Heritage A workshop on the Rue des Étuves, since 1962. Nocturne has never employed more than nine watchmakers at once. The workshop keeps the shutters drawn during assembly — gold dust settles more evenly out of direct light, and old habits are rarely explained twice. Every watch that leaves the bench is entered by hand into a single ledger, still kept in the same drawer as the first entry, dated the fourteenth of March, 1962.
